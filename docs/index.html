1Community view

Following storm runoff downstream

Rain that falls in the headwaters passes through a long sequence of ecosystems before it reaches the communities below. The community view visualizes the above sequence for any community in Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley Regional District.

2Upstream ecosystems

Seeing what these ecosystems already do

This view visualizes each watershed in four different ways: what it holds back today, what its loss would send downstream, how many communities benefit from its retention, and how far that contribution travels.

  • With ecosystems

    The runoff a watershed absorbs during the storm, given its cover, soil type and slope. This view shows existing retention.

  • Without ecosystems

    The extra runoff the same storm would send downstream if the natural landcover in each watershed were cleared.

  • Communities reached

    How many communities benefit from a single watershed's retention. A high count means the benefit reaches multiple communities, sometimes crossing more than one boundary.

  • Distance downstream

    How far a contribution travels before it fades. This measure is focused on orienting the user, showing which ecosystems sit nearest the communities they serve.

3Supply & demand

Putting it all together

Narrowing to Metro Vancouver and Fraser Valley Regional District communities, this view matches the ecosystem services provided upstream against the dissemination areas most exposed within the floodplain. A weighting tool lets you adjust the model inputs to your own priorities.

Critical facilities and population are trial features. Neither takes part in the model behind these rankings.
